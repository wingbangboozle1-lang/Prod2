The work behind it

Executive summary

A buyer-first affiliate publication with a clear evidence trail. This summary separates what is published from what still needs first-party data or an owner-controlled account.

What is live in the project

The Crisp Counter is a static, data-driven editorial shop covering five owner-submitted kitchen products: three air-fryer formats and two meat-thermometer workflows. Its front page leads with products and buyer questions; the source register, intent records, open questions, suggestions, decisions, compliance notes and measurement plan remain available from the “The work behind it” navigation group and footer.

Scope boundary: this repository does not currently run an AI agent, identify trending or high-converting products, query Amazon's private reporting, publish automatically, or optimize campaigns from conversion data. Search-volume and popularity claims are not made. The existing automation builds and validates a static publication; ideas for later research and measurement are queued on the Suggestions page.

What is intentionally blank or absent

  • Prices, availability, ratings and review counts: not published. The governing Program Policies source and the recorded policy decision are linked from Compliance (S-POL, D-P11); do not backfill from memory or old snapshots.
  • Product-level commissions, revenue and conversion forecasts: blank or absent unless obtained from authorized first-party reporting. No score or forecast is inferred.
  • Hands-on performance claims: none. The content compares listed specifications and buyer trade-offs, not products tested by the publication.
  • Traffic-demand claims: none. Guide titles are editorial hypotheses, not statements about search volume, trends or popularity.

Publication and decision support

The shop includes a product shelf, category pages, a comparison, a buying guide, question-shaped articles, a seasonal editorial calendar, RSS, sitemap, authored-byline and a source/method page. The guide queue is a 14-day planning cadence, not a claim that articles will rank or attract traffic. The Suggestions page explains candidate paths, evidence, risk and effort; the Open questions page states missing information and how to obtain it before decisions are made.

For organic discovery, the proposed sequence is: verify the Search Console property, submit the sitemap, collect query reports, then choose topics from measured queries rather than instinct (SG-44). Search Console verification and query data are not present yet (Q-P08/F-P13). The campaign ledger is deliberately empty until authorized first-party reports provide timestamp, source, channel, eligible sessions, clicks and reported orders.

Measurement and automation status

The browser-side counter defines five events and stores locally without cookies or identifiers; it transmits nothing unless configured. A self-hosted Node endpoint exists and is tested, but is not hosted. Its event vocabulary currently does not accept three site event names; connecting the two without resolving that mismatch would lose counts (F-P23/Q-P14/SG-41). Do not treat the endpoint as live analytics.

Automated gates build the pages and check disclosure placement, sponsored links, cited facts, internal links, metadata, sitemap/feed coverage, content claims and register cross-references. These checks improve publishing consistency; they do not replace Associates Central testing, legal review, Search Console verification or human editorial decisions.

Important publication risk

GitHub Pages is currently configured to serve the repository root (Pages API: build_type=legacy, source main /, checked 2026-11-20). That exposes repository source and archived files by URL; robots.txt is not access control. The workflow builds the intended docs/ artifact, but it does not change the active Pages source setting. An attempted API update returned HTTP 403, so this session could not switch the setting. The required repository-admin action and evidence are recorded in Flagged for Review (F-P19/F-P20). This is the highest-priority deployment risk; the intended fix is Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions, followed by verifying a source path returns 404.

Next decisions, in order

  1. Resolve the Pages source setting and verify source files are no longer served (F-P19/F-P20).
  2. Test one final Special Link per product in Associates Central and verify the destination and tracking report (F-57/F-58).
  3. Resolve the site/endpoint event-name mismatch before hosting analytics (F-P23/Q-P14/SG-41); then decide and document a privacy-reviewed hosting arrangement.
  4. Verify Search Console and submit the sitemap; only then use measured query reports to select topics (Q-P08/SG-44).
  5. Keep the campaign ledger empty until authorized first-party reports exist; no forecasts or optimization claims before then.
  6. Complete formal brand clearance and other open editorial/licensing questions listed on the Questions and Flagged for Review pages.

The 2026-11-19 publication pass

The reader-facing site now leads with the products: a shop-first front page (product cards before any data), a guides hub with two content clusters and a seasonal desk, ten guide pages on a 14-day cadence, cleaner navigation, RSS subscribe affordances, and a consent-gated question form that sends nothing until its privacy basis exists (D-P07). Nothing moved on the machinery: disclosure above the fold everywhere, tagged rel="sponsored" Special Links only, prices and ratings still withheld, and every published figure still carries its source and read date.
