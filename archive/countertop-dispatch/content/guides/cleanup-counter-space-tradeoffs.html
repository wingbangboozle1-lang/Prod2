How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Every review of a kitchen appliance eventually says “easy to clean” as if it were a feeling. It is not: it is a number of parts, a surface type, and a square of counter that stays occupied all week. Here is how to read those three off a listing before you buy.

1. Count the parts that touch grease

Cleanup time scales with part count. From the official listings in this set:

  • Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry — the listing states “Dishwasher-Safe Parts” directly. One basket, one rack, one pan. Listing
  • Chefman 6 Qt Dual Basket — two baskets (the listing says dishwasher-safe 3 Qt baskets) plus the unit body. Double the drawers, double the basket wash — but both go in the machine. Listing
  • Emeril Lagasse 26 QT — listing includes a crisper tray and a bake pan, plus a door with a window. Dishwasher-safe status is not stated in the fields we captured FLG-P08. Listing

Ask “how many pieces must I wash after a roast?” — not “is it non-stick?”

2. Convert the footprint into counter-weeks

An appliance that stays out costs counter space every day; one that lives in a cupboard costs setup time every use. The listings give external dimensions, so the footprint is arithmetic:

Footprint = depth × width from each official listing, converted at 2.54 cm/in and rounded. The oven-style Emeril takes roughly 55% more counter than the dual basket — that is the price of the cavity.

3. Read “easy to clean” as three separate claims

  1. Removable, washable parts. Look for the words “dishwasher safe” next to a named part (basket, tray, pan), not in a slogan.
  2. A crumb or drip path. Oven-style units list a crumb tray; baskets drip into the drawer floor. Neither is automatic — both are parts you will handle.
  3. Wipe-down surfaces. Stainless steel bodies (both Chefman units state stainless steel) wipe clean but show fingerprints; matte plastic hides them. Cosmetic, but it is what you see daily.

The trade-off, stated plainly

  • Smallest cleanup burden: single basket — fewest parts, smallest footprint (1,111 cm²).
  • Best two-dish balance: dual basket — actually the smallest footprint of the three (1,020 cm²) with two drawers, at the cost of two baskets to wash (both dishwasher-safe per listing).
  • Most capacity, most cleanup: oven style — 1,722 cm², a door and window to wipe, and more included trays to hand-wash unless the listing says otherwise.
