A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Capacity is the number most likely to mislead you. It is real, it is measured, and it still does not tell you whether a basket fits Tuesday’s dinner. This checklist is what to check instead — in the order that takes two minutes on a product page.

The checklist

  1. Read the unit. Quarts (US) and litres appear side by side in this set and they are not the same number: the Emeril listing states both 26 QT and 24.6 L, which are the same volume. If a listing states only one, convert at 1 qt ≈ 0.946 L before comparing.
  2. Split dual-basket totals. “6 quarts” across two drawers is two 3 Qt cavities — the Chefman Dual Basket listing says exactly that. A total is not a single cavity.
  3. Look for the basket depth, not just the volume. Food browns in a single layer. A shallow wide basket beats a tall narrow one for fries; the listings in this set publish external dimensions, which is the honest proxy we use (see the table below).
  4. Check whether the size in the title is this size. The Chefman TurboFry title mentions a 6–9 qt range because the line sells three sizes; the specification table for this ASIN says 8 quarts. The spec table is the one that describes the item you would receive.
  5. Match capacity to portions, not to “family size”. As a rough guide from the models here: 1–2 portions → 6–8 qt; 3–4 portions or tray cooking → 20 qt+; two dishes finished together → dual basket regardless of total.
  6. Then measure your counter. Capacity you cannot reach because the unit does not fit is zero capacity.

What this checklist will not tell you

  • How crispy something gets. That depends on wattage, airflow design and food load — none of which a capacity number captures. Wattage here: 900W stated on the TurboFry listing; the other two listings do not state wattage in the fields we captured FLG-P08.
  • What it costs or how it is rated. Prices and star ratings change by the hour and Amazon’s Program Policies limit how we may display them; the listing link always shows the current values. Why.
