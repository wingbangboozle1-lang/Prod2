What the checked data shows

Four analyses run over the five checked products: counter-space, category structure, commission economics, and verification coverage. Each states its method and links its sources.

1. Counter-space: capacity per centimetre of depth

Counters are lost to depth first. Dividing each air fryer's listed capacity by its listed depth gives a rough “litres per centimetre” efficiency — useful for asking whether the capacity you gain is worth the depth you pay:

ProductListed capacityDepthCapacity ÷ depthFootprintSource
Chefman 6 Qt Dual Basket 6 qt ≈ 5.7 L34.0 cm0.17 L/cm 1,020 cm² S-14
Emeril Lagasse 26 QT 24.6 L42.9 cm0.57 L/cm 1,722 cm² S-15
Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry 8 qt ≈ 7.6 L32.4 cm0.23 L/cm 1,111 cm² S-16

Capacity converted at 1 qt ≈ 0.946 L; depth converted at 2.54 cm/in; both from the dimensions printed on each official listing. The oven wins on ratio because its cavity is a box — but it also occupies 55% more counter than the dual basket. Ratio is a conversation starter, not a verdict: use it with the footprint column, never alone.

Finding A The 26 QT oven packs the most capacity per centimetre of depth and the least per square centimetre of counter — those two facts pull in opposite directions, which is exactly why the chooser asks about your depth before your appetite.
