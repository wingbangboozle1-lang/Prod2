Capacity

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts are a marketing unit. Five checks tell you what a fryer will actually hold — before the box lies to you.

Why “quarts” tells you less than you think

The quart number on the box is a volume rating for the machine’s chamber, not a promise about dinner. What matters is usable volume: baskets have walls, racks steal height, and air needs room to move or you steam instead of crisp. Two machines with the same quart rating can hold very different meals.

So run the checklist below before you trust any capacity claim — including ours. Everything we state about the five products here comes from their official Amazon listing titles, verified 2026-11-19; where the listing doesn’t state something, we leave it blank rather than guess.

The capacity checklist

  1. Count the baskets. A “6-quart dual basket” is two 3-quart pits. That’s the Chefman 6 Qt’s honest geometry — its listing title says two independent 3-quart baskets, not one big one. Great for two dishes, limiting for one huge tray.
  2. Prefer stated dimensions to stated quarts. Dimensions are the number you can actually measure your counter against. We do not copy dimension claims into our comparison table yet — our fetches verified the listings but not their spec tables — so the table shows blanks with a flag instead. When dimension values are verified from the official listings, our counter-space analysis will publish the methodology and the numbers together.
  3. Match the fryer to your food, not to your family size. Family-size claims are unstandardized. A portion of fries that overlaps barely crisps — surface area matters more than depth. Think about the widest single thing you cook, then compare basket floor space.
  4. For oven-style, check rack reality. The Emeril Lagasse 26 QT lists a 26-quart cavity and six included accessories. Accessories change what “26 quarts” means in practice — racks split the cavity; a rotisserie setup eats clearance. Count what’s included before imagining the load.
  5. Beware “serves N” claims. There is no official standard behind them. Treat any “serves 6” as marketing until you have read the basket dimensions.

Capacity per centimetre of counter depth

This is the number a listing cannot give you: usable quarts divided by the counter depth the machine actually occupies, including door swing and shake clearance.
