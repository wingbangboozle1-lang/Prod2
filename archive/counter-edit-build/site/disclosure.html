Disclosure policy

How this site earns, what the labels mean, and where our obligations come from — with links to the primary sources so you can read them yourself.

The affiliate relationship

The Counter Edit participates in the Amazon Associates Program. Outbound product links to Amazon.com carry our Associates tracking ID (shopnow047c-20). If a reader clicks one and makes a qualifying purchase, Amazon may pay us a commission from the purchase price. The reader’s price is never affected. This disclosure appears above the fold on every page of this site and again in the footer.

What the labels on our links mean

Every outbound Amazon link on this site is marked rel="sponsored nofollow noopener". The sponsored marker is a public, machine-readable statement that the link is paid — see Google’s guidance on qualifying outbound links. For readers, the meaning is simpler: those links are advertising, and they are labeled as such.

Where the obligations come from

  • Amazon Associates Program Policies — the Special Link, disclosure and participation requirements this site operates under.
  • FTC: Disclosures 101 — clear-and-conspicuous placement (that is why the strip is above the fold, not buried).
  • FTC: Endorsements, Influencers and Reviews — the endorsement context we review every page against.

What we do not claim

  • We do not claim any submitted short link is compliant or non-compliant — its status is unresolved and tracked as flag F-02. Reader-facing links use tagged amazon.com/dp/ Special Links built from observed listing ASINs.
  • We do not publish prices, ratings or stock states — those live on Amazon and change daily.
  • We do not claim popularity for any product without a dated snapshot link (decision D-19).

Privacy on this site

The measurement layer stores aggregate events in your browser only — no cookies, no advertising identifiers, no third-party scripts. If a self-hosted, aggregate-only analytics endpoint is switched on, it receives page-view counts, outbound-click counts and scroll-depth buckets — never identities. The metrics plan documents every event we collect.
