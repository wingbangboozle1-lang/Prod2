Who it suits

One dish, narrow counter, big batch

  • Houses where dinner is one thing at a time. A single 8 qt XL basket batches a family portion in one go instead of two 3 qt runs.
  • Shallow counters. 12.75″D is the least counter depth of the three fryers — 0.63″ shallower than the dual-basket, 4.15″ shallower than the French-door oven.
  • Reheaters and refiners. 4-in-1 as listed: air fry, bake, dehydrate, frozen — plus the 450°F Hi-Fry option for a crisper finish.
  • People who set-and-forget. The listing notes an LED shake reminder and automatic shut-off.

Who it doesn't suit

Two-zone dinners and tray cooking

  • Two components, different settings, same finish time. One basket means one zone; that job is the dual-basket's, and the two-baskets guide does the math.
  • Sheet-pan meals. A basket is not a rack; tray cooking lives in the oven format.
  • Anyone choosing by the first price they see. Variant families change price by option; prices are not published here (F-02).

Measure first: the counter math

Listed at 12.75″D × 13.5″W × 10″H: about 172 in² of footprint (depth × width) and 0.63 qt per inch of listed depth. Footprint tells a different story than depth: the dual-basket actually covers less counter (≈158 in²) while needing more wall clearance. Both numbers are derived from listed dimensions, and the full formula is in the counter-space assessment. The listed height is 10″ — hold it against your own cabinet clearance the same way.
