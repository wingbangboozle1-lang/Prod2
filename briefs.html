The work behind it · Decisions

Six decisions, and what each one is waiting for

Every open item on this site is not an engineering gap — it is a question only the account holder can answer, in a browser, in about a minute each. Here they are in the order they should be made, with the evidence already on file and the cheapest test that closes each one.

This page exists because a list of forty flags is not a plan. The Flagged for Review page records what is irregular; the Open questions page records what is unknown; the Suggestions page records what could be built. None of those tells you what to do next. This one does, and it refuses to move a brief off the list until the answer is recorded with a date.

How to use this page

Read the six-row table. Pick a brief. Do the test in its "cheapest way to find out" box — each one costs under five minutes and none of them requires this project to change anything first. Then send back the answer, and the row behind it gets resolved with the date on it. Nothing here asks you to trust a conclusion; every brief shows the row that would prove it.

Ordered by irreversibility, not by impact: the briefs that cost an account or a rename come before the ones that cost a commit. Each brief below lists what is already verified, with the register row that proves it, then what is still missing and the cheapest test that would close it.

What this site already got right, so the briefs are short

Five things were settled by reading official pages rather than by asking you, and they are worth stating plainly because they remove work from every brief below:

  • All five products are real, live and correctly identified. Every listing was re-read on 2026-11-20 and every document title matches the register character for character, including each category breadcrumb. See the method page for read dates.
  • No price and no star rating is published anywhere, because Amazon permits a published price only when Amazon serves it in the link or it arrives through the licensed API (F-02, F-P17). The refusal is deliberate and already complies.
  • Every outbound product link carries the tracking tag and is marked rel="sponsored", and the build fails if either is ever missing.
  • The Associates disclosure sits under the masthead on every page and again in the footer, in the first-person sentence the Operating Agreement §5 prescribes.
  • No page claims anyone cooked with these machines. There is no hands-on claim on the site, and the gate fails the build if one appears (D-06).

Those five are why the briefs are six questions rather than sixty. What remains is genuinely outside what this project can see.

The briefs

Brief 1 · B-01

Is the Associates account enrolled, and does shopnow047c-20 actually attribute?

Every revenue claim on this project rests on this one answer. Until it is answered, no measurement on the site can be tied to money.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • The tracking ID shopnow047c-20 was supplied by the owner and its format matches Amazon's documented US Store ID pattern (ends in -20). Q-01 · S-TAG
  • All five outbound product links on the site carry tag=shopnow047c-20 and rel="sponsored", and the gate fails the build if either is missing. Compliance page · tools/checks.py links section
  • The five owner-submitted short links carry no tag parameter. F-03
  • The supplied short links resolved to their intended ASINs on every read from 2026-11-14 to 2026-11-19, and to the Amazon homepage on three reads on 2026-11-20. F-P24 · F-P27 · third_pass_2026_11_20_arena_15730819
  • Amazon's Operating Agreement states Special Links must use the Associates ID Amazon assigned, with the tag as a parameter in the URL. S-OA §2(b) (Participation Requirements), read 2026-11-20

What is not known

  • Whether the ID is registered, active, or inside the 3-sale/180-day enrolment requirement — none of that is visible from outside Associates Central.
  • Whether a click from this site reports in the Associates dashboard. Nothing on this site can test that; only the owner's console can.
  • Whether the five short links are compliant Special Links. Their status is unresolved (FLG-003, carried) and this project has never claimed they are invalid.
  • Whether the short-link misdirection on 2026-11-20 is a change in Amazon's short-link service, a fetch-tool artefact, or a per-token rotation. Two same-day reads disagreed on two of five links (F-P24), so neither is treated as the answer.

The cheapest way to find out

Open ONE product's tagged link from the live site, buy nothing, and check that the click appears in Associates Central → Reports with the correct tag. Amazon's own guidance is one link per product before scaling (F-58).

Who can answer it: The owner, inside Associates Central, in a normal logged-out browser.

Deciding this unlocks

  • Any statement that this site earns
  • The campaign ledger (it accepts only authorised first-party rows)
  • Every conversion recommendation on the Suggestions page

It does not block

Publishing. The site can ship and be indexed while this is open; it simply cannot claim revenue.

Brief 2 · B-02

Can this site legally use the name it currently publishes under?

The site ships as 'The Crisp Counter'. A working brand that has not been cleared is a rename risk on every page, every feed URL and every social card at once.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • The name 'The Crisp Counter' was adopted on the owner's anti-slop directive as a working brand, explicitly without formal clearance. D-P18 · F-P21
  • A preliminary search found no conflicting kitchen-appliance brand in the first page of results. That is not a clearance. data/p2/settings.json brand.name_basis (2026-11-20)
  • Amazon's Program Policies forbid Amazon or affiliate trademarks, or variants or misspellings of them, in a domain name, subdomain, tag/Associates ID, or username. The name contains no Amazon mark. S-POL (Trademark Guidelines / suitability), read 2026-11-20
  • The site has no domain of its own; it is served under a github.io subpath. Q-19

What is not known

  • Whether 'The Crisp Counter' is registrable or already registered as a mark in the relevant classes.
  • Whether any existing food publication uses a confusingly similar name.
  • Which jurisdiction the owner would register in.
  • Whether the owner wants a domain at all — that decision changes the cost of renaming by an order of magnitude.

The cheapest way to find out

Search the exact phrase plus 'kitchen' and 'air fryer' on USPTO's public search and on a registrar; record the date and result. Two names can be carried in parallel in this register until one clears.

Who can answer it: The owner, with a trademark search (USPTO TESS or a paid clearance search) and a registrar check.

Deciding this unlocks

  • A domain purchase
  • Print/social identity
  • Any press or outreach that names the site

It does not block

Everything else. The brand is one line in settings.json and a rebuild; reversal is recorded as cheap in D-P18.

Rows behind this brief: F-P21  ·  Questions: Q-19

Read it yourself: USPTO trademark search · Associates Program Policies (Trademark Guidelines section)

Brief 3 · B-03

May this site publish a price at all?

The single largest reader-trust feature a shopping page can have, and the single easiest way to breach the Operating Agreement.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • Amazon permits a site to show prices and availability only if (a) Amazon serves the link in which that price is displayed, or (b) the data comes through the Creators/PA API under the licence. S-POL §2(b) (Links on Your Site), read 2026-11-20
  • Every clear price string visible on the five listings belongs to a neighbouring ASIN in an 'also viewed' carousel, not to the product on the page. F-P17 · third_pass_2026_11_20_arena_15730819.pricing
  • The build fails if a dollar figure or a star-rating string appears in any published page. tools/checks.py claims section
  • The site therefore publishes 'Check price on Amazon' instead of a number, on all five products. index.html shelf · D-P11

What is not known

  • Whether the owner is enrolled in the Creators API / PA API — the only route that would make a published price lawful today.
  • Whether Amazon will serve a price inside the Special Link on this site's traffic.
  • What a compliant refresh cadence would be if API access existed (the licence sets this; the project has not read it).
  • Whether the trading position is unchanged — the rate table and pricing rules are Amazon's to change, and this project has set no re-read reminder.

The cheapest way to find out

Log into Associates Central and check whether the Creators API or PA API is available on the account. If it is not, the answer is 'no price', and the current build is already correct.

Who can answer it: The owner (API enrolment), then a dated read of the API licence terms.

Deciding this unlocks

  • Any price-comparison table
  • Any 'best value' recommendation
  • Any deal or Black Friday surface (the season the calendar is entering)

It does not block

Everything the site currently does. The refusal is already the shipped state and is explained on every card.

Rows behind this brief: F-02 F-P17 F-P26  ·  Questions: Q-27

Read it yourself: Associates Program Policies — §2(b), prices and availability · Creators API / Product Advertising API (enrolment gate)

Brief 4 · B-04

Where does the click counter actually send its numbers?

Whether the site can learn anything from its own traffic, or whether every future topic decision stays a guess.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • The site defines exactly five events — page_view, outbound_click, guide_read_75, category_click, feed_click — and the shipped client sends exactly those five. data/p2/events.json · assets/js/measure.js
  • The collector answers 400 to none of them; the vocabulary mismatch that silently dropped two thirds of the events was fixed and is now a hard gate failure. F-P23 (resolved) · D-P21 · tools/checks.py measurement section
  • The counter sets no cookies, stores no identifiers, and until an endpoint is configured it writes only to the reader's own browser and transmits nothing. That is the shipped state. privacy.html · data/p2/settings.json analytics_endpoint_note
  • GitHub Pages is static and cannot host the collector; the Pages API returns 403 to this session, so even the Actions switch is an owner action. F-P19 · INT-07

What is not known

  • Which host the owner wants (any container, VM or function that speaks HTTPS).
  • Who pays, and how much — no cost figure is published because none has been obtained.
  • The retention period and who reads the aggregate.
  • Whether the owner wants server-side measurement at all, or would accept browser-local counts as the permanent ceiling.

The cheapest way to find out

Run server.mjs locally (npm start), set the endpoint in settings.json, open one page, and confirm one aggregate row lands. That proves the loop for the cost of a laptop minute.

Who can answer it: The owner.

Deciding this unlocks

  • Any measured topic choice
  • Any statement that the site is measured
  • Feeding real queries into the guide queue

It does not block

Publishing. The site is honest that it currently measures nothing centrally.

Rows behind this brief: F-19  ·  Questions: Q-23 Q-P17

Read it yourself: The event contract as shipped · Measurement page

Brief 5 · B-05

Should the whole repository stop being readable on the live host?

Reader trust and a smaller attack surface. Right now anyone can read the registers, the generator and this coordination file at the site's own address.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • Pages serves this repository in legacy 'main /' mode, so everything committed to the root is fetchable, including data/ and tools/. F-P20 · INT-07
  • The Pages REST API returns 403 to this session on any attempt to switch to Actions mode, so the switch needs a repository admin. F-P19 · recorded three times, re-confirmed 2026-11-20 (PUT /pages → 403)
  • robots.txt disallows /Prod2/archive/ but cannot stop a human or a scraper reading anything else. robots.txt
  • The published artifact docs/ contains only rendered pages, assets and the feed; no register is inside it. tools/checks.py exposition section

What is not known

  • Whether any reader has actually hit a source file (no server logs are available from Pages).
  • Whether the owner prefers Actions-mode Pages, a separate gh-pages branch, or a domain in front.
  • Whether the repository should be private — which would conflict with using it as the Pages source without a paid plan.

The cheapest way to find out

Open https://wingbangboozle1-lang.github.io/Prod2/COORDINATION.md in a logged-out window. If it renders, the exposure is live and confirmed.

Who can answer it: A repository admin (the owner) in Settings → Pages.

Deciding this unlocks

  • Publishing the campaign ledger's real rows (they would be world-readable)
  • Anything the owner would not want indexed
  • The 'no source in the artifact' promise actually being true at the host level

It does not block

The reader experience, which is already correct.

Rows behind this brief: F-P19 F-P20  ·  Questions: Q-P08

Read it yourself: The exposure, as described on the Flagged page · GitHub Pages source settings (admin only)

Brief 6 · B-06

Is there a legal way for this site to show the actual product?

The difference between a publication and a set of diagrams. Right now every product image is a drawn illustration of the format.

What is known — with the row that proves it

  • The six illustrations were drawn for the site, are generic to the product format, and every card is captioned 'illustration, not the product photo'. D-P12 · README (Imagery)
  • Amazon listing photography is Program Content, and using it is conditioned on the IP Licence, which this project has not reviewed. Q-P05 · S-POL IP License section
  • The listing pages do expose image URLs (m.media-amazon.com), which is exactly why the licence question has to be answered before one is copied. third_pass_2026_11_20_arena_15730819 (listing fetches)

What is not known

  • Whether the IP Licence permits linking to or hot-linking Amazon-hosted images from an Associates site — the single test is to open a SiteStripe image link on one enrolled product and read the licence text that accompanies it.
  • Whether the owner has any original photography.
  • Whether a commissioned drawing set is preferable to licensed photography on cost grounds — no quote has been obtained.

The cheapest way to find out

In Associates Central, open SiteStripe on one product, pick an image link, and read the licence terms shown there. One minute, and it settles whether photography is available at all.

Who can answer it: The owner (SiteStripe access), then a dated read of the IP Licence.

Deciding this unlocks

  • Any page that looks like a shop rather than a guide
  • Open Graph images that show a product
  • Paid or affiliate-network partnerships that require product imagery

It does not block

Publishing, which currently uses drawings and says so.

Rows behind this brief: —  ·  Questions: Q-P05 Q-P16

Read it yourself: Associates Program Policies — IP License and Usage Requirements · Imagery policy as published

What is deliberately not on this list

Four things look like decisions and are not, because the answer is already fixed by an official source or by the site's own rule:

  • "Which product is best?" — Not answerable. Nobody here has used them, and a "best" badge is exactly the claim this site refuses to make (D-06). The site ranks by fit to a described cook instead.
  • "Should we show prices to look more like a shop?" — Not until B-03 is answered. Looking more commercial is not a defence under §2(b).
  • "Should we add more products?" — Not yet. Two of the six briefs are about the links and the brand that carry the current five; adding a sixth product multiplies an unresolved answer.
  • "Should we automate the optimisation?" — Not before B-01 and B-04, because there is nothing to optimise against until clicks are attributed and counted.

The one-line version

Do B-01 first — one tagged click, read in Associates Central. It is the only brief whose answer changes what every other page on this site is allowed to say.
