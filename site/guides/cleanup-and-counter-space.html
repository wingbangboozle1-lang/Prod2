How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The best air fryer is the one you will actually use and clean. Learn how to weigh cleanup effort against the counter space it takes.

Two habits kill air fryer use: the unit takes the whole counter, and cleaning feels like a second chore. Decide both before you buy. A unit you cannot leave out is a unit you will not use; a basket you have to scrub by hand will end up in the cupboard.

For cleanup, the key question is whether the removable parts are dishwasher-safe. Basket-style models usually have one nonstick drawer and a tray. Oven-style models add a door, racks and grease catch, so there are more parts. Look for nonstick coatings and a grease catch that is easy to empty.

For counter space, measure the footprint and the clearance you need around it. Our counter-space analysis compares capacity per unit of counter depth so you can see what each model really costs on your bench.

If you want the simplest clean-up in a compact footprint, the Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry has a single dishwasher-safe basket. If you want a door-style cooker with more parts to wash, the Emeril Lagasse 26QT French Door trades more cleaning for more cooking room.

Questions people ask

Can I leave an air fryer on the counter?

If it fits with clearance and you use it often, yes. Measure depth and the space above it before deciding; some models need room for hot air at the rear.

Which parts usually need hand washing?

The heating element and the unit's body should stay dry. The basket, tray and grease catch are usually the dishwasher-safe parts. Check the listing for 'dishwasher-safe'.

Does a larger unit always need more cleaning?

It has more surfaces, yes. A single-basket model is generally the least fuss to clean day to day.
