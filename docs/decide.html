Two minutes Four questions, one answer

Tell us how you cook. We'll name the one to buy.

No score, no star rating, no "top pick" badge. Answer what you actually do in the kitchen and we will name one of the five tools, show the listed fact the answer rests on, and tell you what would flip it to a different machine.

How this answer is built

Every question below maps to a value printed on an official Amazon listing — counter depth in inches, listed capacity in quarts, basket counts, probe count, heat rating. Nothing is inferred from a price, a rank or anyone's opinion. If a listing does not state a value, the question that relies on it is skipped rather than guessed, so a blank on the listing becomes a blank in the answer (F-02).

1. What do you actually want it for?

This decides whether we are routing to an air fryer or a thermometer.

2. How do you usually cook?

Two different dishes on one plate, or one thing at a time?

3. How deep is the counter space you can give it?

Measure front-to-back, from the wall to the front edge, and leave the clearance the manual asks for behind it. Guesstimating here is the single most common way a purchase goes wrong.

4. How many people are you feeding?

Listed capacity is volume, not servings — but it is the only honest number on the page.

5. What are you cooking most often?

This decides whether you need a number in a second, or a probe you leave in for hours.

6. Where will it be used?

One of the two thermometers has probes rated for the air fryer; the other is a probe you hold.

What this page will not do

It will not tell you a machine is "the best". It will not show a price, a star rating or how many people "bought this today" — the register forbids all three, and two of them are not ours to publish (F-02, F-15). It will not recommend something we have cooked with, because we have not cooked with any of them (about the site).
