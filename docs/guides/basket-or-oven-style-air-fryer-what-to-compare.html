Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

By the Editorial team (no named author supplied, FR-11)

Two designs show up in the listings we checked: a basket air fryer, and a countertop air-fryer oven with a door. The listing titles name the form. They do not say which one suits a given kitchen, and this guide does not rank them.

What the listings say

  • Chefman 6-quart dual-basket air fryer: “2 Independent 3Qt” baskets, as printed in the title.
  • Emeril Lagasse 26QT French door air fryer toaster oven: “24 One-Touch Preset Options” and “Six Cooking Accessories Included”, as printed.
  • Chefman 8-quart TurboFry air fryer: “4-in-1” with a “450°F Hi-Fry Option”, as printed.

Questions to answer before you compare

  1. How many people do you cook for, and do you cook two dishes at once? Two baskets and a single chamber are different layouts, and we have not tested either.
  2. How much counter depth and door clearance do you have? We did not extract listing dimensions (FR-08), so measure your space and read the dimensions section on each listing yourself.
  3. Which functions do you actually use: air fry, bake, broil, dehydrate, toast? Check the function list on each listing, not the number of presets.
  4. Who will clean it, and which parts does the listing describe as dishwasher-safe?

What we cannot tell you

We have not tested these appliances, and no ratings or performance results appear on this site. Anything printed on a listing is the seller’s or manufacturer’s statement, not ours.
