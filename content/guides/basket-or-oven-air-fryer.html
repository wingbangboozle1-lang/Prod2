Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Nobody wakes up wanting an appliance. They want crispy fries without heating the kitchen, or a way to cook two things at once on a weeknight. The shape of the air fryer decides how well it does those jobs — so compare the shapes, not the preset counts.

There are two shapes on the market, and this issue’s set contains one clear example of each plus a compact in-between:

  • Basket style — a drawer you pull out, shake, and slide back. Fast to preheat, easy to shake mid-cook, smallish cavity. Example in this set: the Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry (8 qt, 32.4 cm deep).
  • Dual-basket style — two independent drawers. Two dishes, two temperatures, same moment. Example in this set: the Chefman 6 Qt Dual Basket (two 3 Qt baskets, 34.0 cm deep).
  • Oven style — a hinged door and a rack cavity, sometimes with French doors. Fits a tray, a pizza, a small chicken; takes the most counter. Example in this set: the Emeril Lagasse 26 QT French Door (24.6 L, 42.9 cm deep).

The right comparison is not “which has more presets” — it is “which shape matches the meals I cook twice a week?”

The four axes that actually decide it

1. Capacity against the food, not the box

Listed capacity is a cavity number. What matters is how your food fits: fries want a single layer, wings want space to not stack, a tray wants width. The 26 QT oven swallows a tray the baskets cannot take; the dual basket wins when the meal is two dishes that finish together. Our capacity checklist has the two-minute version.

2. Depth, because counters are measured in depth

Every kitchen has a wall and an edge, and the appliance lives between them. From the official listings in this set:

3. Cleanup, which is really “how many parts touch grease?”

A basket is one drawer and one rack. An oven is a door, a rack, a crumb tray, and a window you will wipe. Both Chefman units list dishwasher-safe parts; the Emeril listing includes a crisper tray and bake pan. The question to ask is not “non-stick?” but “how many pieces must I wash after a roast chicken?” Our cleanup guide works through it.

4. What you cook twice a week

  • Weeknight sides, one dish at a time → basket is enough.
  • Protein plus side, finished together → dual basket.
  • Trays, pizza, whole chicken, reheating yesterday’s roast → oven style, if the depth fits.
  • Mostly seared meat and you care about doneness more than crisping → you may want the thermometer first: see the two checked probes.

Before you decide

  1. Measure the counter depth between wall and edge. Anything deeper than the number above will not fit.
  2. Name the two meals you cook most. Match the shape to those, not to a holiday roast you cook twice a year.
  3. Count the parts you are willing to wash. Then run the three-question chooser and see which of the three it picks for you.
