Compare the drawer, not the badge. "Air fryer" describes how a machine cooks - fast moving hot air - not what shape it takes, and the two shapes in your search results cook differently because their heat comes from different places. A basket air fryer blasts heat down into a small sealed drawer. An oven-style air fryer pushes heat across a wide cavity from behind a door. Once you know which problems each shape solves, the comparison list almost writes itself.

What the drawer does that the oven can't

A basket surrounds food on all sides, so small batches crisp fast and evenly with very little attention to arrangement. The sealed drawer also dumps steam out through a vent instead of onto your food, which is why frozen fries in a basket come out drier than the same fries on a tray. The ritual that matters is the shake: pulling the drawer mid-cook to turn what the airflow can't reach. Some listings make that ritual easier to remember - the 8-quart Chefman TurboFry on this site's register, for example, ships with an LED shake reminder as a listed feature (read from its official listing page, 2026-11-16). That's the drawer's whole personality: quick, sealed, shake halfway.

What the door-front oven does that the drawer can't

An oven-style air fryer trades the drawer's speed for range. Two or three rack levels mean a tray of vegetables and a fillet cook at once. Toast, bake and broil modes mean it can replace - not just sit next to - a toaster oven. Rotisserie shows up here, not in drawers. And you can watch through the door: the French-door format on our register, the Emeril Lagasse 26QT, lists French doors and rotisserie among its features (official listing, read 2026-11-16). The cost is real, though: a wide body, a deeper counter reach, and typically more surfaces to wash than one basket and one tray.

The five comparisons that actually matter

  1. Usable space, not box capacity. A drawer's usable area is one flat layer; an oven's is two or three racks. The capacity checklist breaks down how to read both.
  2. Heat position and the shake. Downward heat + sealed drawer = shake ritual. Rear or top heat + racks = turn-the-tray ritual. Pick the ritual you'll actually do.
  3. Depth on the counter. Oven-style bodies run deep. Before you fall for a 26-quart cavity, run your counter through the capacity-per-centimetre method.
  4. Cleanup surface count. A drawer is two parts. An oven is racks, a tray, a crumb shelf and the door glass. If weeknight washing is the bottleneck, count the parts now, not after the first cook.
  5. The second job. If the machine must also toast breakfast or rotisserie a chicken, that's an oven-style job. If it must also fit in a cupboard, that's usually a drawer's job.

The five-minute tie-breaker

Write down the one meal you cook most weeks. If it's frozen snacks, nuggets, or two-serving mains, a drawer will do it with less counter real estate and less washing. If it's family trays, batch meal-prep, or anything that needs two temperatures at once, the oven style earns its footprint - or consider the middle path: a dual-basket drawer, which gives you two independent baskets in one body (our register's 6-litre Chefman dual basket works this way, as listed). Then measure your counter and your cupboard, and you're done.
