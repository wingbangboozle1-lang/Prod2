The most common air-fryer regret isn't the cooking - it's the arrival day, when the box meets the counter and something doesn't fit. Five minutes with a tape measure and this page's checklist prevents all of it.

Minute 1: measure depth at the plug side

Depth is the killer dimension. Measure the counter from edge to backsplash (or wall), then subtract the machine's listed depth and the clearance the manual requires behind it. Listings print depth first: our register's three machines list 13.38, 16.9 and 12.75 inches of depth respectively (official listings, read 2026-11-16). The French-door oven's extra three inches sound small until they're your prep space. For the honest version of this comparison, use the capacity-per-centimetre method.

Minute 2: measure height at the shelf, too

If the machine will ever live in a cupboard or under a cabinet, measure that opening's height and compare it to the listed height plus the handle or lid overhang. Height is where storage plans quietly fail - handles and rising hinges are usually unlisted, so leave a finger's width of slack.

Minute 3: swing the door in your head

Drawer machines need frontal clearance for the full drawer pull - roughly the drawer's own depth in open space. Door machines need hinge swing on one or both sides. Stand at the spot, mime the motion, and look for the collision: the wall, the kettle, the faucet, the person who opens the dishwasher at the same moment.

Minute 4: ask the outlet what it can give

Two questions. First, is there an outlet within the cord's reach - cords are short on purpose, and extension leads are the wrong answer near a heat source. Second, what else shares that circuit: a microwave on the same circuit as a high-wattage air fryer can trip a breaker at the worst moment. If the answer is "the microwave," find another outlet before you order, not after.

Minute 5: check the clearance rules in the manual

Every manufacturer states required clearance around the machine - sides, back, top. The numbers vary by model, so this is a manual check, not a general one: read the specific figure for the specific machine before finalizing the spot. The "free corner" behind the toaster usually isn't free once the clearance is priced in.

Write it down

By now you have four numbers on a sticky note: available depth, available height, swing space, outlet situation. Every listing you consider must beat those numbers, not just look good in photos.
