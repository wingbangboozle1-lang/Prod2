Four questions a sale week can't answer

Sale week makes every machine look urgent. The discount changes what you pay — it doesn't change which format fits your counter, how much capacity your meals need, or which workflow you'll actually keep. Four questions, answered from the listings this site follows.

Discount weeks are loud by design: timers, percentages, one-day framing. None of it answers the questions that decide whether an air fryer earns its counter space or a thermometer stays in the drawer. The four questions below do — and unlike a discount, they stay true on the other side of the sale. Every figure cited here was read from an official listing on a dated pass (the receipts sit in the product register), and wherever a product link does appear on this site, it is a clearly marked paid link.

1. Which format fits the kitchen I actually have?

Three formats sit on this site's shelf — dual-basket, oven-style, single-basket — and each one negotiates with your counter differently. Basket drawers are compact but vertical; the French-door oven takes the deepest listed footprint of the three at 16.9 inches of counter depth and its title lists 24 one-touch preset options and six cooking accessories to work with; the single basket is the quiet weeknight machine. Measure the counter before the sale starts, not while a timer is running. The format decision is decoded in basket vs oven-style.

2. How much machine do my meals actually need?

Capacity labels don't compare themselves across formats — quarts describe volume, not people. If the discount tempts you toward the biggest number on the shelf, run the sanity check first: how many quarts do you actually need?, then the longer capacity checklist for cross-format comparisons. The listed capacities of all three air fryers sit in the product register, each with the date it was read.

3. What does the listing tell you that the discount can't?

A sale price is one line. The listing tells you what you're actually buying: what's in the box (P2 lists six included cooking accessories, read 2026-11-20), which parts go in the dishwasher (P3's basket is listed dishwasher-safe, read 2026-11-20), and what the presets actually do once the novelty wears off — the multi-function questions count them down honestly. This site deliberately publishes no prices and no star ratings (F-02): the discount in front of you is yours to evaluate; the specs underneath it are what we can help with.

4. Which workflow will I actually keep?

A well-priced machine is a bad buy if its workflow doesn't match your week. Two drawers earn their counter space when you genuinely cook two dishes at once — the batch math runs the numbers. A thermometer is really two tools: a spot-check instant-read and a leave-in wireless watch (which for which cook) — pick by the minute that worries you. None of that changes when a timer is running. It's exactly what a timer won't tell you.

What we could not verify

How good any specific sale actually is. This site publishes no prices and tracks no price history (F-02), and nobody here has tested these machines hands-on (D-06). Deal depth, stock and availability during a sale week are outside what a dated listing read can confirm — check the live listing before buying, and treat any countdown as a mood, not a measurement.

The one-sentence answer

Settle format, capacity, counter fit and workflow first — then a discount is just a nicer price on the machine you already chose, not the thing doing the choosing.
