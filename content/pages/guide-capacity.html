A no-hype checklist for comparing air-fryer capacity

Capacity numbers are the most quoted and least comparable figure on any listing. This checklist makes them mean the same thing across formats — five minutes, no spreadsheets.

Quarts measure volume. Cooking happens in shapes. A 26 qt oven and a 6 qt dual-basket machine are not "26 versus 6" — they're a tray versus two drawers, and the numbers describe containers, not dinners.

The checklist

  1. Read the total listed volume — and then forget it briefly. P1: 6 qt total (2 × 3 qt). P2: 26 qt (24.6 L as listed). P3: 8 qt. Numbers collected from the official listings; each in the comparison table with its source link.
  2. Count the zones. Volume split across two baskets is not the same as volume in one cavity. P1's two 3 qt zones each take less than P3's single 8 qt basket — and neither takes what P2's rack does.
  3. Ask what the largest single item is. The question that kills capacity confusion: "will a whole chicken / a 12″ pizza / a sheet of vegetables fit?" A basket answers by width and depth of its drawer; an oven answers by rack dimensions. If the listing doesn't state it, it stays blank — we don't guess.
  4. Check the unit wording. Listings mix quarts and litres, sometimes in the same product: P1's title says 6 Quart while its spec table has said 6 litres (F-P04), and P2 gives both 26QT and 24.6 liters. Compare like with like, and prefer the title's format claim for the number people quote.
  5. Check the variant. Some listings are size families: P3 belongs to a 6/8/9-quart family with four GTINs on its page (F-08). The cart, not the listing, decides which one you get.

What the numbers say about real meals

A practical, non-magical reading of the three air fryers here:

  • 1–2 people, one dish at a time: P3's 8 qt single basket is generous, and its 12.75″ listed depth is the gentlest on counters.
  • 2–4 people, two components: P1's 2 × 3 qt is built for exactly this — and its listed Sync Finish addresses the "one side's done first" problem.
  • 4+ people or tray cooking: P2's 26 qt listed interior is the only one here that takes a spread-out tray; that's a different cooking style, not just a bigger one.

The rule this site follows

Publish what the listing states, dated and sourced — and say so when a figure is missing. Capacity claims here are never rounded up, converted loosely, or "estimated for a family of four." Blank means unconfirmed (method).
