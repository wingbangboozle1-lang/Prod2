How do you know the turkey is done?

Not by the clock, and not by the pop-up timer folklore. The USDA answer is one number — 165°F (74°C) in the bird — and one instrument: a food thermometer. The only real question left is which of the two thermometers we follow fits the way you cook the big meal.

The number is not ours. FoodSafety.gov's cook chart states poultry — "All: whole bird, breasts, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets, sausage, and stuffing inside poultry" — at 165°F (74°C), under the instruction to "always use a food thermometer" (chart last reviewed November 21, 2024; source register S-USDA-COOK). What follows is how the two listed tools answer "is it there yet?" — with listing facts only, read 2026-11-19.

Check it: the one-second answer

  • P4 is a spot-check instrument (P4) — a listed 1-second thermocouple read, NIST-certified ±0.5°F accuracy as listed, IP67 waterproof body, auto-rotating LED display.
  • How it fits the day: you open the oven, probe the bird in a few places, and know. The listed ±0.5°F accuracy claim is tighter than the single-degree scale the cook chart itself works in — but the certified value is a listing claim, not a lab result from us.

Watch it: the walk-away answer

  • P5 is a leave-in instrument (P5) — three listed probes rated to 1000°F, five listed sensors, WiFi range as listed, app alerts plus hub audio reminders.
  • How it fits the day: the probe goes in before the bird does, and the answer comes to you while you are doing everything else a house full of people requires. The listing also marks the probes air-fryer-safe — relevant if the side dishes are running in one of the machines below.

Which one for the hosting table

The trade-off is attention, not accuracy. A spot-check costs seconds at the oven door and nothing else; a leave-in costs setup up front and then protects the cook's evening. Households that roast once a year tend to have the first conversation twice; households that cook big meals often can have both conversations at once — one probe in the bird, one instant-read for the rest. The instant-read vs leave-in guide and the accuracy guide go deeper on each.

What we could not verify

Whether either thermometer holds its listed accuracy in real kitchens, how the app behaves on a crowded WiFi network, and how many seconds "1 second" really is. The 165°F figure is federal guidance; everything about the instruments is listing copy. We did not test either unit (D-06).

The one-sentence answer

165°F in the thickest part of the bird is the USDA's finish line; a one-second thermometer tells you when you check, and a wireless probe tells you before you have to.
