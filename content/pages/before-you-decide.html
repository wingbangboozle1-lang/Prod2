The work behind it

Before you decide anything

Eleven decisions are waiting on you. None of them can be made from what this project currently knows, and none of them need a meeting — each one needs one fact that only you can obtain, in a place this build cannot reach. Below: what we know, what is missing, how it gets found, and what changes the moment it arrives. Everything else on this site keeps working while you decide.

The short version

Four facts unlock almost everything: (1) whether shopnow047c-20 is live in your Associates account, (2) where the analytics endpoint will run, (3) whether you control a domain, and (4) what you are willing to say about the brand name before it is cleared. Everything on the suggestions board that is still proposed is downstream of one of those four. Numbers 1 and 3 also decide whether any commission is ever attributed at all — that is not an optimisation, it is the difference between a shop and a catalogue.

1 Are we earning commission at all?
Tag registration		 The supplied tag shopnow047c-20 matches the documented US Store ID shape and is published on every buy link (F-57). Whether it is registered and active is not observable from outside Associates Central. Enrollment status of the account, and whether a test click appears in the report (Q-01). No page claims otherwise. Also unblocks the one-link-per-product validation test (F-58).
2 Does any reader behaviour get measured?
Analytics endpoint		 The counter (assets/js/measure.js) and the collector (server.mjs) both ship, agree on all five event names, and are tested. No server is deployed, so settings.analytics_endpoint is empty and the site sends nothing (F-19). Where it runs and who pays (Q-23, Q-P17). Pick any HTTPS host, run node server.mjs, set the endpoint in data/p2/settings.json, rebuild. Turns on the five defined events. Without it, every "what should we publish next" answer is instinct, not evidence (SG-34).
3 Can search engines see us properly?
Search Console		 Canonicals, sitemap, robots and unique titles are all built and gated. The property is not claimed (F-P13), so nobody can see which queries arrive. Who verifies, and which method (Q-P08). The HTML-tag token goes into settings.json and the build emits it; then submit the sitemap. Real queries replace guesses for the next topic. This is the only path to the "measured, not instinctive" goal in the brief.
4 Is the brand name ours to keep?
Trademark clearance		 "The Crisp Counter" ships on a preliminary search only (F-P21). No Amazon Mark appears anywhere; the build fails if one does. Whether the name collides with a live mark (Q-19). A formal search — USPTO TESS — or counsel. This is the one item whose cost rises with time, not effort. Either the name is ratified, or the rename is a single setting plus a rebuild. Deliberately reversible.
5 Do we ask readers for anything?
Reader capture		 The form is built and deliberately disconnected — it stores nothing (F-P14). No consent basis has been chosen. Whether an email may be collected, and under what consent text (Q-47). You choose: email capture with a stated basis, or questions-only. The second option ships with no further work. Capture on = a channel you own. Capture off = no list, and the question-to-guide pipeline waits.
6 May we publish prices or ratings?
Snapshot policy		 Prices and star ratings are blank on every page by rule (F-02). The reason is attribution: on live reads, the $ figures sat inside "customers also viewed" carousels and belonged to other ASINs (F-P17). Whether a dated-snapshot discipline is acceptable to you, and who maintains it (Q-27). Your call. A dated snapshot means a re-read schedule and a visible "observed on" date, or it stays blank. Adopting it (SG-05) adds a maintenance obligation. Keeping the rule costs nothing and is defensible. Both are honest; pick one.
7 May we show real product photos?
Imagery rights		 Every image on the site is a drawing, labelled as one. Product photography needs the licensed channel (SiteStripe or the API), which is not reachable from here (Q-P16). Whether the enrolled account exposes a usable image, and under which terms. Open one SiteStripe image link on one enrolled product and read the terms shown. One test settles it. Real photographs would raise trust and conversion. Drawings stay until the licence is checked — not because they are worse, but because unlicensed photography is a compliance problem.
8 Is the served site the right site?
Pages exposure		 The live host serves the repository root, not the built artifact. Source files are reachable at public URLs (F-P20, F-P19). This sandbox cannot change it (the API returns 403 for this integration). One dropdown on your side: Settings → Pages → Source. Choose GitHub Actions. The workflow already builds and gates docs/. Removes the exposure and deletes a duplicate copy of every page. Two minutes, fully reversible (SG-46).
9 Do the submitted links still point at these products?
Short-link drift		 On 2026-11-20 the five submitted short links (https://link.amazon/B0f3GKalj and four others, quoted in full on the products page) did not resolve to their listings — four landed on the homepage, one returned 500 (F-P24). The tagged /dp/ links the site actually publishes are built from verified ASINs and are unaffected. Whether this is a fetch-tool artifact or a real change at the short-link service. Open each short link in a normal browser once. Note where it lands and the date. Nothing reader-facing changes either way — the short links are never clickable here (F-03). It only settles what the register records.
10 What should publish next?
Cadence		 A fourteen-day cadence and a queue of question-shaped topics exist. The queue order is editorial, not measured, and says so (the calendar register). Measured queries — which needs #3 and #2, not another opinion (Q-P02). Nothing to decide now. Ship the queued topics on cadence; switch to measured selection once 14 days of data exist. Turns topic choice from taste into evidence. This is the payoff of items 2 and 3, not a decision of its own.
11 How long should a piece be?
Depth standard		 Two long reads run ~1,200–1,300 words, each carrying inline sources and a "what we could not verify" block. There is no published length rule, and word count is a poor control anyway — filler satisfies it. Whether you want a standard at all, and if so which one (Q-P15). Choose a sourced-claims floor (e.g. cited claims per 100 words) instead of a word count, or decline a standard. Either a gate that filler cannot pass, or no rule. The current state — quality without a number — is viable but unfalsifiable.

If you only do four things, do them in this order

  1. Confirm the tag (row 1). Nothing else in this project produces income until this is true. It is a login and one test click.
  2. Fix the Pages source (row 8). Two minutes, removes a real exposure, and needs no technical judgement from you.
  3. Claim Search Console (row 3) and name an endpoint host (row 2). Both turn the site from a guess into a measurement. They can happen in either order; together they take under an hour.
  4. Start the name search (row 4). It is the only item on this list whose cost grows if it waits, because every page, feed item and social card carries the name.

Rows 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11 are real decisions with real consequences, but none of them block publishing, and none of them get better with urgency. They can wait until the four above are settled.

What this page deliberately does not do

  • It does not estimate revenue. No conversion rate, commission rate or traffic forecast is published anywhere on this site, because none has been observed. F-01 records why the product-level commission rate is blank: no official source maps these ASINs to a commission category.
  • It does not rank products by earnings. Ranking by an unverified commission rate would be a number invented to look decisive.
  • It does not guess an answer to fill a cell. Every "what is missing" cell above is missing because the fact is unreachable from here, not because nobody looked. The read dates and the tool used for each are on the method page.
  • It does not decide for you. Rows are ordered by what they unblock, not by what we would prefer you to choose. Where this project has a recommendation, it is on the suggestions board with its evidence, and it says "proposed", not "decided".

How to answer a row

Quote its number and say what you found. That is enough — the registers, the gate and the pages follow from a recorded answer. A row with no answer stays open, is published as open on the Flagged for review page, and blocks only itself. Nothing here will quietly assume an answer on your behalf, and a later session will not invent one to make a table look complete.
