Buying guide

How to choose: format first, capacity second, counter always

A buying guide that starts from your kitchen instead of a brand name. Six sections; each ends with the question that decides it.

1. Format is the real decision

Brands compete on features. You will live with a format. The three air fryers on this site are three different answers to "how do you cook":

  • Dual-basket (P1): two independent 3 qt zones that can finish together. Best when your meals have two components that want the same temperature, not the same time.
  • Oven-style (P2): rack cooking behind French doors, 26 qt listed interior. Best when you cook on trays — vegetables spread out, a small roast, toast.
  • Single-basket (P3): one 8 qt basket, the shallowest footprint here. Best when you cook one thing well and your counter is spoken for.

Deciding question: what shape is most of your food — two separate things, one tray, or one basket-load?

Walk through it in three questions on the chooser, or read basket vs oven in detail.

2. Capacity, honestly

A "6 quart" dual-basket machine and an "8 quart" single basket are closer than they sound: the dual splits its volume into two 3 qt zones, and no zone fits a whole chicken. Quarts also convert differently on different listings — P1's title says 6 Quart while its spec table has said 6 litres (F-P04).

Compare capacity by asking, in order: total listed volume → number of zones → the largest single item each zone accepts. The capacity checklist turns that into a five-minute routine.

Deciding question: what is the largest single thing you actually cook?

3. The counter always wins

Every one of these machines is a standing reservation on your counter. The listed depths here run from 12.75″ (P3) to 16.9″ (P2) — and depth is just the start: lids, doors and baskets need room to open.

The counter-space analysis divides listed capacity by listed footprint so you can see what each square inch buys. It's a formula, not a verdict — your counter is the verdict.

Deciding question: how many inches of depth can you spare, measured — not remembered?

4. Cleanup is a spec, not a surprise

Listings tell you more about cleanup than reviews usually do: P1's baskets and P3's basket are listed dishwasher-safe; P2 ships six cooking accessories and cleans like a toaster oven — more parts, more counter-time after dinner.

Material matters too: nonstick baskets, stainless bodies, crumb trays. The cleanup and counter-space guide runs through what to check.

Deciding question: how many parts are you willing to wash on a Tuesday?

5. Multi-function, minus the fantasy

Presets are a list of temperatures and times with a name attached. Twenty-four of them (as P2's listing states) sounds like twenty-four reasons to buy; in practice most kitchens rotate through four or five functions. Before paying for versatility, ask which functions you used last month in some other appliance — because that is the job the new machine must absorb.

The multi-function questions guide has the full checklist.

Deciding question: which listed functions replace something you already do — and which are decoration?

6. Doneness: the second machine

Half of good cooking is knowing when food is done, and that is a different purchase. Two formats live on this site:

  • Instant-read (P4): one-second spot checks, ±0.5°F certified accuracy as listed. In and out.
  • Wireless leave-in (P5): probes that stay in while you stay away, alerts to your phone, 1000°F heat-safe as listed.

What accuracy claims actually mean, and which fits which cook.

Deciding question: do you need to know the temperature now, or the whole time?

What we deliberately left out

Prices, star ratings and "best-seller" badges. They change faster than honest verification, and the first two can be had from the listing in one click. What stays on this page is what stays true between reads. (F-02, D-19)
