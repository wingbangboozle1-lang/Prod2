Six decisions, and what each one is waiting for

Every open item on this site is not an engineering gap — it is a question only the account holder can answer, in a browser, in about a minute each. Here they are in the order they should be made, with the evidence already on file and the cheapest test that closes each one.

This page exists because a list of forty flags is not a plan. The Flagged for Review page records what is irregular; the Open questions page records what is unknown; the Suggestions page records what could be built. None of those tells you what to do next. This one does, and it refuses to move a brief off the list until the answer is recorded with a date.

How to use this page

Read the six-row table. Pick a brief. Do the test in its "cheapest way to find out" box — each one costs under five minutes and none of them requires this project to change anything first. Then send back the answer, and the row behind it gets resolved with the date on it. Nothing here asks you to trust a conclusion; every brief shows the row that would prove it.

What this site already got right, so the briefs are short

Five things were settled by reading official pages rather than by asking you, and they are worth stating plainly because they remove work from every brief below:

  • All five products are real, live and correctly identified. Every listing was re-read on 2026-11-20 and every document title matches the register character for character, including each category breadcrumb. See the method page for read dates.
  • No price and no star rating is published anywhere, because Amazon permits a published price only when Amazon serves it in the link or it arrives through the licensed API (F-02, F-P17). The refusal is deliberate and already complies.
  • Every outbound product link carries the tracking tag and is marked rel="sponsored", and the build fails if either is ever missing.
  • The Associates disclosure sits under the masthead on every page and again in the footer, in the first-person sentence the Operating Agreement §5 prescribes.
  • No page claims anyone cooked with these machines. There is no hands-on claim on the site, and the gate fails the build if one appears (D-06).

Those five are why the briefs are six questions rather than sixty. What remains is genuinely outside what this project can see.

What is deliberately not on this list

Four things look like decisions and are not, because the answer is already fixed by an official source or by the site's own rule:

  • "Which product is best?" — Not answerable. Nobody here has used them, and a "best" badge is exactly the claim this site refuses to make (D-06). The site ranks by fit to a described cook instead.
  • "Should we show prices to look more like a shop?" — Not until B-03 is answered. Looking more commercial is not a defence under §2(b).
  • "Should we add more products?" — Not yet. Two of the six briefs are about the links and the brand that carry the current five; adding a sixth product multiplies an unresolved answer.
  • "Should we automate the optimisation?" — Not before B-01 and B-04, because there is nothing to optimise against until clicks are attributed and counted.

The one-line version

Do B-01 first — one tagged click, read in Associates Central. It is the only brief whose answer changes what every other page on this site is allowed to say.
