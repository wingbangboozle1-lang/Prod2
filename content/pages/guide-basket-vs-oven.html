Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

The question isn't "which is better." It's "which cooking habit do you have." Here's what the listings actually state when you line the two formats up.

An air fryer is a small convection oven with opinions. The basket type holds food in a perforated drawer where air hits it from everywhere at once; the oven type puts food on racks behind a door, like a countertop oven. Everything that matters to you follows from that one difference.

1. Food shape beats capacity numbers

A basket wants food in a loose layer — that's where "shake the basket" comes from. An oven wants food on trays. If most of your cooking is nuggets, fries, wings: basket. If it's vegetables spread across a sheet pan, toast, a small roast: oven.

The listings here say it plainly: P1 (basket) leads with "2 Independent 3Qt Nonstick Dishwasher Safe Frying Baskets"; P2 (oven) leads with "Extra Large Interior, Six Cooking Accessories Included" — trays and racks, read from its listing (2026-11-19).

2. Two zones versus one tray

The dual-basket format splits the difference: P1's two 3 qt zones let you run two foods at different settings and finish them together (a listed "Sync Finish"). The oven doesn't split — it spreads. Its 26 qt listed interior takes a tray that a 6 qt basket physically cannot.

Compare instead: how often do you cook two things that want different settings? If the answer is "most meals," dual-basket. If it's "one tray of mixed things," oven.

3. The counter math

Baskets are taller than they are deep; ovens are deep, period. The listed depths here: P1 at 13.38″, P3 at 12.75″, P2 at 16.9″ (full counter-space analysis). An oven-style machine also needs door clearance — the French doors swing out.

4. Heat-up and speed

None of these listings states a preheat time or a cook time, so this is a trade-off to weigh rather than a measured difference. What the numbers do say is the size of the space: P1 and P3 are baskets holding a few quarts each, P2 is a 26 qt rack cavity. Less air to heat means a shorter wait; more room means one run instead of two. Which rhythm matches your household is the real comparison.

5. Cleanup, honestly

P1's two baskets and P3's basket are listed dishwasher-safe. P2's listing states racks, a tray and six included cooking accessories; it does not state a cleaning method for them, so treat that as unknown rather than as hand-washing. The honest contrast is a stated dishwasher-safe claim on one side and an unstated one on the other — and more parts to wash either way.

The one-sentence answer

Cook two things at once? Basket format. Cook one big tray? Oven format. Cook one thing well with no counter to spare? A large single basket. That's the whole decision — the rest is details in the comparison table.
