Preheat, shake, or don't: which air fryer rituals change your food?

Three rituals follow air fryers around: preheating, shaking the basket mid-cook, and giving the food an extra minute at the end. Two of them are built into these machines as listed features. The third nobody here can settle for you — and we will say why.

The listed facts below come from the three official listings, read 2026-11-19 (feature tables 2026-11-14). This is a specification publication (D-06): no unit was tested on this site, so every claim here is a claim the listing makes.

Shake: the one ritual the machines ask you for

  • P3 lists an LED shake reminder (P3, source S-P3) — a light that tells you to shake the single 8 qt basket mid-cycle. Its listing also carries an auto-shutoff and a programmable timer.
  • P1 skips the shake and splits the load instead (P1) — two independent 3 qt baskets with a listed "Sync Finish" feature and a listed "+2Min" button. Two smaller zones are the machine's answer to the even-cook problem a shake usually solves.
  • P2 has no basket to shake (P2) — the 26 qt toaster-oven format cooks on racks. Its listed controls are a timer, temperature control and programmable presets.

So the shake question is really a format question in disguise: a big single basket prompts you to shake, two small baskets divide the load in advance, and a rack oven removes the ritual entirely. The two-baskets-or-one guide runs the batch math on that trade-off.

Preheat: the ritual no listing here commits to

None of the three listings states a preheat step in the facts we registered. P1 and P3 list "Hi-Fry" as a finish option — 450°F on P3, as listed — and P2 lists 24 one-touch presets whose timing behaviour we could not read from the listing text. Whether preheating changes your results on any of these machines is a behaviour claim, and none of us has tested it (D-06). If the answer matters to you, it lives in the manual, not in this register.

The last minute: the ritual you can buy

P1's listed "+2Min" button and both Chefmans' listed "Hi-Fry" option are the same instinct as a product feature: a burst of extra heat or time at the end of the cook. The listings promise the option. What it does to your food is, again, a behaviour — unverified here.

What we could not verify

Whether preheating improves results on any of the three machines, whether a mid-cook shake changes browning, and what Hi-Fry or "+2Min" actually do to food. Those are behaviours, and this is a specification publication — no hands-on claims appear here. The listed features are facts; their effects are not.

The one-sentence answer

Shake is a real ritual the listings engage with — one machine reminds you, one designs around it, one removes it — while preheat and the extra minute are promises you can only check against the manual or your own dinner.
